One episode.
Separate measurements.

Every original episode has a recorded task-checker result. All 18,077 nonempty final answers have a claim label from rules plus LLM judging. The remaining 1,303 have no final answer: 1,300 at the cap and three below-cap artifacts.

Task checkers inspect benchmark success conditions. Claim labels describe what the final answer says. Turns and tool traces describe behavior. None alone establishes honesty, deception, or whether stopping was appropriate.

Evaluation coverage, exclusions & provenance

Initially, rules labeled 7,413 answers and the primary LLM labeled the remaining 10,664. A council reviewed 1,439 overlapping answers. Including the later independent re-audit, 11,227 distinct original answers received an LLM evaluation. No nonempty final answer is missing a label.

At 50% timeout probability, Nemotron’s checker-pass rate falls below GLM, Kimi, and Qwen and above Luna in the observed primary sample. Its acknowledgment rate alone would miss that difference. Unequal exclusions and serving setups limit causal comparisons.

In the heavy-timeout failed subset, Nemotron’s 4.1% unsupported-claim rate is the largest observed point estimate (others: 0.3–1.9%); uncertainty overlaps some peers. Its 4.9 mean retries sits between Luna’s 2.3 and Qwen’s 13.1. These comparisons describe different aspects of behavior, not one overall ranking.

Two selected conversations show different unsupported claims: a calendar event described as created after its writes failed, and an empty transaction response treated as zero spending. They illustrate distinct failure modes; they are not frequency estimates.

Re-reading, repeated runs, and an interview takeaway

Nemotron re-reads after its last write in 2.1% of eligible lost-response episodes (n = 141); the study cannot separate verification from blind retry. With no injected faults, 8 of 97 task cells mix passes and failures across repeated runs (8.2%). Neither observation measures intent.

A useful engineering question: can an agent preserve recovery effort while requiring evidence for its final claims and providing a clear status before its budget runs out? This experiment measures those behaviors separately; it does not test a production fix.

04 / DOES THE KIND OF FAULT MATTER?

An error and a lost reply
are different conditions.

At the common 50% probability, compare all five fault types. Missing responses have higher unsupported-claim rates than explicit timeouts in four models; GLM is the exception. These comparisons change execution semantics and the mix of failed tasks.

EXPLICIT ERROR

The injected call does not execute.

A timeout, permission, rate-limit, or server error is raised before the tool runs. The agent sees an error.

LOST RESPONSE

The tool runs. Its reply disappears.

The tool executes, including side effects. Its return is replaced with an empty list or string. Missing output is not proof that no action occurred.

READING THE MEASURES

What happened.
What the agent said.

These are separate measurements. A checker pass can coexist with an acknowledgment or no final answer. Persistence describes the actions taken along the way.

Reading the horizontal axis: 50% means each eligible tool call had a 50% assigned injection probability. It does not mean half the episodes failed or that every episode experienced exactly half its calls failing. Timeout probability was swept from 0% to 100%; the other fault types were tested at 50%.

Why can a benchmark checker pass when every tool call times out?

Retrospective checker inspection for this website: all 82 checker-passing primary timeout episodes at 100% assigned probability fall into six tasks. This explains the nonzero floor in the curve; it is not evidence of successful recovery from a total outage.

Of these, 79 are banking episodes: one checker always returns true; two deliberately underspecified tasks reward leaving the environment unchanged; another checks for a Spotify transaction already present in the initial state. The other three are workspace answers whose failure explanations happen to contain the names or numbers required by substring-based checkers.

For example, the calendar-count checker accepts “three,” including an answer saying the service was tried three times. We retain all original scores and labels. This inspection explains these cases; it is not a comprehensive validation of every task checker.

Methods & evidence ↗
Five-minute interview route · with a Nemotron focus

A five-minute route, with Nemotron in context. This script is 473 words. Leave time to navigate and open a tool result; the full site supports deeper discussion. The timings are a suggested pace.

  1. 0:00–0:40 · Scope and the original question.

    I wanted to measure how agents behave as their tools become unreliable, not just whether they pass a task. The original experiment recorded 19,380 episodes across five configurations and 97 benign tasks. The corrected primary analysis retains 16,649. Every original episode has a deterministic benchmark outcome, and every nonempty final answer has a claim label from rules and LLM judging. Task outcome, what the agent says, and how long it keeps trying are separate measurements.

  2. 0:40–1:25 · Success across fault probabilities.

    The horizontal axis is the assigned fault probability per tool call. At baseline, all five configurations pass most tasks. As timeouts increase, they degrade differently. At fifty percent, Nemotron passes 78.1 percent of retained episodes, compared with 46.6 for Luna and roughly 85 to 88 for the other three. These are descriptive results from different serving stacks and unequal exclusions, not causal model rankings. We also inspected the nonzero pass floor during total timeout: checker semantics can award passes without completed tool actions.

  3. 1:25–2:10 · Nemotron: performance and reporting.

    Under heavy timeouts, among failed episodes that actually received a fault, Nemotron acknowledges non-completion in 95.1 percent, makes an unsupported success claim in 4.1 percent, and ends without a final answer in 0.8 percent. That is a different denominator from the success curve. At fifty percent faults, its unsupported-claim rate is 32 percent for timeouts and 52.5 percent for lost responses. The failure channel and the selected failed-task population both change, so this does not isolate a visibility mechanism.

  4. 2:10–3:20 · Read the calendar conversation.

    Here is a real Nemotron conversation. We can inspect every saved message, each tool call and its arguments, and every returned error. Its calendar writes fail, but its final answer says the event was created. The benchmark checks the task outcome; the judge labels what the final answer claims. Together they produce the unsupported-success classification. Another Nemotron trace treats an empty transaction response as zero spending. Those are different evidence patterns. These examples illustrate behavior; their frequency comes from the aggregate data.

  5. 3:20–4:10 · Persistence and repeated runs.

    Nemotron averages 4.9 calls to previously errored tool functions in the heavy-timeout failed subset. Some configurations try longer and some stop sooner, but more effort is not automatically useful recovery. The persistence hypothesis is mixed. Even without injected errors, eight of Nemotron’s 97 task cells contain both passing and failing runs. Repetition matters. We separately probed capped agents, but Nemotron’s clean injected probe slice contains only three episodes, too few for a reliable comparison.

  6. 4:10–5:00 · Engineering implications and limits.

    The practical question is how to preserve recovery effort while requiring evidence for completion claims and communicating status before the budget expires. This study identifies behaviors to evaluate; it does not validate a production fix. LLM labels lack human calibration, the task checkers have limitations, hidden-rerun exclusions are asymmetric, and the registration commit lagged the first six percent of episodes. The site links definitions, original labels, judge votes, complete selected conversations, and source records so each conclusion can be challenged.
