01 / BEHAVIOR UNDER FAILUREPRIMARY ANALYSIS

Failure is an outcome.
Reporting it is a behavior.

When a task fails, agents can acknowledge non-completion, claim success, or reach the step limit without a user-facing answer. These behaviors differ sharply across the five tested models.

91.5%

of Qwen ablated’s failed, dosed episodes at high timeout rates ended at the cap without output.95% task-cluster interval: 88.0–94.5% · n = 574

A missing answer
is itself an outcome.

The contrast survives a capability check. Restricting to tasks each model succeeded on in at least three control repetitions changes each headline rate by less than one percentage point.

A model-level description, not an ablation effect. Qwen ablated has no matched, non-ablated control on the same serving stack. The design cannot attribute this pattern to removing safety training.

02 / THE BASELINE MATTERSTIMEOUT-RATE SWEEP

The loudest failures
get the clearest response.

Unsupported success claims are frequent among ordinary, uninjected failures. At total injected timeout outage, none were observed in the failed, dosed sample. The pattern is consistent with failure visibility mattering; visibility was not independently isolated.

40.7–94.1% is conditional on failure. It is not the share of all tasks with false success claims. Baseline denominators are small: 17–53 failed episodes per model, with wide intervals.

Zero observed is not zero possible. At p = 1.0 the point rate is 0% for all five models; the published upper interval bounds are approximately 0.7–1.0%. The failed population also changes as p increases.

No answer yet.
Unable to answer?

The original cap censored what would happen next. A follow-up probe revisited all 1,300 capped episodes: first a user nudge during the outage, then a tools-off question for agents that still did not answer.

does not establish

to report.

Among GLM, Kimi, and Qwen stage-B answerers, 95.0–98.3% acknowledged non-completion after tools were removed.

This is evidence of reporting capability in a selected subset. It does not tell us what an agent would do with more turns and no user intervention.

05 / THE INVESTIGATION EVOLVEDDESIGN → DISCOVERY → QUALIFICATION

The harness needed
scrutiny, too.

This began as a 24-hour project. Gaps surfaced during execution and review. The response was to document incidents, reanalyze the existing evidence, narrow claims, and run a targeted follow-up within the available budget.

  1. START

    Start small, then expand with capacity.

    The original 16-task design expanded to all 97 tasks. A temporary Kimi scope exception was reversed to preserve parity. These changes preceded aggregated analysis, although live progress had been visible.

    Registration and scope history ↗
  2. DURING RUN

    Distinguish infrastructure failures from behavior.

    Workers died without a confirmed root cause. Endpoint failures and below-cap empty generations received a documented one-time rerun policy. Atomic records and ID-based resumption preserved completed episodes.

  3. ~16 HOURS

    Freeze 19,380 episode records.

    The dataset completed with zero duplicate records and zero recorded harness errors in the frozen corpus. Three recurring below-cap artifacts remained excluded from analysis.

  4. ~33 HOURS +

    Build the report, then challenge it.

    AI-assisted adversarial reviews exposed denominator problems, hidden benchmark reruns, judge circularity, and overstrong construct names. The preserved reviews show what changed and what remains unresolved.

  5. FOLLOW-UP

    Reanalyze, re-audit, and probe the censored cases.

    Primary tables exclude hidden reruns and oversampled repetitions. Stronger claim re-audits and all 1,300 capped-episode probes add evidence. These layers do not constitute a clean rerun of the full original experiment.

DISCOVERY 01

One episode could hide
multiple attempts.

The injector counted more tool calls than the saved transcript contained. A benchmark rerun path affected 901 episodes across three models. Replay fidelity failures provided additional evidence.

Response: exclude affected primary episodes; disclose the asymmetry and replay limit.

Bug assessment ↗
DISCOVERY 02

Assigned failure is not
experienced failure.

An episode assigned p > 0 may receive no injected error at all. Pooling those cases with dosed failures blurred baseline capability failures and behavior after injection.

Response: require realized dose for injected conduct rates; show the uninjected baseline.

Correction record ↗
DISCOVERY 03

Judge agreement is not
human calibration.

A judge sat on its own validation council, and truncated text could hide qualifications. Even unanimous votes could be wrong. Human labeling was infeasible in the weekend budget.

Response: use different judges, full captured text, control cases, and explicit uncertainty bounds.

Re-audit and remaining bias ↗

Registration nuance: the commit landed 46 minutes after the run began. 94% of episodes post-date it; excluding the early 6% moves no reported headline rate by more than 0.9 percentage points. Two clerical dates are preserved and explained in the timeline reconstruction.

07 / WHAT THE EVIDENCE CAN CARRYBOUNDARIES, NOT FINE PRINT

Useful findings.
Bounded conclusions.

The evidence is about these five models, on this scaffold, under these synthetic conditions. The site preserves the final reports and makes their qualifications visible.

01

One scaffold and benchmark

97 benign English office tasks, a short loop budget, and uniform independent faults. Production outages, long-horizon agents, multimodal inputs, and prompt-injection attacks are outside scope.

02

Models and stacks are confounded

Each model used one serving path. Gateway checkpoints are not pinned by the study. There is no matched non-ablated Qwen control and no causal safety-training conclusion.

03

Labels retain uncertainty

No human calibration. Judges may share bias, acknowledgment and ambiguity overlap, and some original inputs were truncated. Success-claim labels alone do not prove deception or intent.

04

Follow-up is a different protocol

The probe adds a user message and later removes tools. Replay exclusions are uneven. It cannot recover the unobserved counterfactual of simply granting more turns.

The practical lesson: evaluate what an agent reports alongside what it achieves—and instrument the evaluation well enough to notice when either measurement is misleading.
