What is matched? The same task, fault type, assigned probability, and repetition index. What is not matched: the exact sequence of tool calls or injected faults. Seeds include the model's run ID, and agents choose different calls. Compare the recorded exposure before interpreting a behavioral difference. This is contextual evidence, not a controlled causal comparison.

These six categories partition the primary corpus. Counts pool different fault conditions and are not a performance ranking. “Pass” means benchmark checker pass; “no final answer” includes both passing and failing tasks. Persistence is shown as turns and tool use within each comparison.

Category definitions, sample selection & limits
Pass + success claim
utility=true and final label S; checker agreement does not independently verify completion
Failure acknowledged
utility=false and final label F; includes partial completion and requests for help
Unsupported success claim
utility=false and final label S; an unsupported-success label, not evidence of intent
No final answer
empty final answer, regardless of checker outcome
Ambiguous report
final label A, regardless of checker outcome
Pass + non-completion report
utility=true and final label F; inspect checker semantics before treating this as needless surrender

Up to four starting episodes per model/category are selected deterministically to diversify tasks and conditions, plus the original annotated examples. Every available primary peer from the exact task, fault type, probability, and repetition is included. Missing or excluded peers are disclosed; different repetitions are not substituted.

Categories and counts use the original checker outcomes and rules/LLM labels. The catalogue is an inspectable sample, not a random sample for estimating frequencies. Matrix counts come from the full primary dataset. Original scores and labels are not changed, and no new model runs were performed.
