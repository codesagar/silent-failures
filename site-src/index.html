HOW THE EVIDENCE FITS TOGETHER

One episode.
Separate measurements.

Every original episode has a recorded task-checker result. All 18,077 nonempty final answers have a claim label from rules plus LLM judging. The remaining 1,303 have no final answer: 1,300 at the cap and three below-cap artifacts.

Task checkers inspect benchmark success conditions. Claim labels describe what the final answer says. Turns and tool traces describe behavior. None alone establishes honesty, deception, or whether stopping was appropriate.

Evaluation coverage, exclusions & provenance

Initially, rules labeled 7,413 answers and the primary LLM labeled the remaining 10,664. A council reviewed 1,439 overlapping answers. Including the later independent re-audit, 11,227 distinct original answers received an LLM evaluation. No nonempty final answer is missing a label.

Primary population: 19,380 − 1,920 supplementary repetitions − 3 below-cap artifacts − 808 remaining hidden reruns = 16,649. Of 901 hidden reruns, 91 overlap the supplement and two overlap the artifacts. This corrects the frozen report’s overlap wording; the primary total and inclusion rules are unchanged. The primary set comprises 11,044 timeout/control episodes and 5,605 episodes in the other fault-type arms. A record being evaluated does not make it eligible for every analysis.

The 1,300-episode cap follow-up is a separate population. Original labels remain unchanged. LLM labels lack human calibration; original judge inputs were truncated. Evaluation instruments and limitations ↗

Ordinary failure can be misreported.

Among failed baseline tasks with no injected faults, success-claim rates range from 40.7% to 94.1%. Those estimates use only 17–53 failures per model, with substantial uncertainty.

Total outage does not mean better agents.

Observed unsupported claims fall to zero among failed, fault-exposed total-timeout episodes. An agent may acknowledge failure or provide no final answer. Low claim rates alone are not a safety score.

04 / DOES THE KIND OF FAULT MATTER?

An error and a lost reply
are different conditions.

At the common 50% probability, compare all five fault types. Missing responses have higher unsupported-claim rates than explicit timeouts in four models; GLM is the exception. These comparisons change execution semantics and the mix of failed tasks.

EXPLICIT ERROR

The injected call does not execute.

A timeout, permission, rate-limit, or server error is raised before the tool runs. The agent sees an error.

LOST RESPONSE

The tool runs. Its reply disappears.

The tool executes, including side effects. Its return is replaced with an empty list or string. Missing output is not proof that no action occurred.

05 / DO REPEATED RUNS AGREE?

One run is not
the whole behavior.

With no injected faults, 5.2–15.5% of model–task cells contain both passing and failing runs across retained repetitions. This is useful even though it confirms an expected limitation of single-run evaluations.

Do agents re-check after writing?

In the lost-response arm, re-reading after the last write occurs in 1.9–29.5% of episodes containing a write, depending on model. Re-reading may reflect verification or another retry; this design does not distinguish intent. Read the measure and results ↗

07 / SEPARATE FOLLOW-UP · CAPPED EPISODES ONLY

After silence,
ask what happened.

The original experiment came first. A later probe targeted its 1,300 capped episodes. Among selected Qwen nonresponders, removing tools and asking again elicited acknowledgment in 531 of 559 answers: 95.0%.

What follows: silence within the original budget does not establish inability to acknowledge non-completion. The intervention changed both the question and tool availability; it is not a validated production fix.

For Nemotron: only three episodes remain in the main clean, injected probe slice. That is descriptive evidence, not a reliable model-level comparison. Its original-run behavior is the stronger basis for discussion.

READ THE EVIDENCE AT THE RIGHT LEVEL

The outcome.
The report.
The path between them.

Evaluate these together. A capable agent can misreport failure; a cautious report does not guarantee task completion; a missing answer need not mean nothing happened.

One benchmark, five configurations, synthetic faults, and a fixed scaffold. The evidence describes these runs and does not establish deceptive intent or an effect of removing safety training.
