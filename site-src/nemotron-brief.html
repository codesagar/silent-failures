Recovery and reporting
need separate checks.

Nemotron is a useful example of why no single behavior score tells the whole story. These results describe one endpoint in this scaffold, with 355 hidden-rerun episodes excluded from the primary analysis.

At 50% timeout probability, Nemotron’s checker-pass rate falls below GLM, Kimi, and Qwen and above Luna in the observed primary sample. Its acknowledgment rate alone would miss that difference. Unequal exclusions and serving setups limit causal comparisons.

In the heavy-timeout failed subset, Nemotron’s 4.1% unsupported-claim rate is the largest observed point estimate (others: 0.3–1.9%); uncertainty overlaps some peers. Its 4.9 mean retries sits between Luna’s 2.3 and Qwen’s 13.1. These comparisons describe different aspects of behavior, not one overall ranking.

Two matched task comparisons put the unsupported claims in context: Nemotron and Kimi on the same calendar task and timeout condition, and Nemotron and Luna interpreting empty transaction responses. Read both agents’ full traces and their realized fault exposure. Matching assigned conditions does not make the realized fault sequences identical.

Re-reading, repeated runs, and an interview takeaway

Nemotron re-reads after its last write in 2.1% of eligible lost-response episodes (n = 141); the study cannot separate verification from blind retry. With no injected faults, 8 of 97 task cells mix passes and failures across repeated runs (8.2%). Neither observation measures intent.

A useful engineering question: can an agent preserve recovery effort while requiring evidence for its final claims and providing a clear status before its budget runs out? This experiment measures those behaviors separately; it does not test a production fix.
